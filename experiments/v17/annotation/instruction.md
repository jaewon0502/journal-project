# Frozen annotation clarification: proposition change

This is a bounded annotation regression, not a writer trial, article edit, factual-accuracy improvement experiment, or re-scoring of v17. Replace only `original_meaning_loss` with `original_proposition_changed` in a future annotation contract. Do not emit the old field in new annotations. Existing observations, judgments, actions and scores remain immutable. Other existing factual_error, necessity and attributed-value distinctions retain their meaning; no new editorial checklist or repair gates are introduced.

Read only this instruction and items.json. Each O/C/S/Q record is a complete *local annotation packet*, explicitly not a full article or full source. Compare O to the supplied pre-repair C, never to a later applied repair. Do not browse, inspect original audit files, access reference-withheld.json, freeze.json or prereg.md, or consult another judge. No edits, patches, scores, restoration decisions or publication decisions are requested.

## Replacement criterion (fixed before judge calls)

`original_proposition_changed`: yes / no / unknown / not_applicable.
- yes: within the stated target, C alters, negates, removes, or newly restricts a proposition expressed in O, including its quantity, scope, condition, force, or speaker attribution. An added exclusive definition of an existing O term counts as a scope change even when O did not spell out the excluded detail. A correction of a false O proposition still counts as yes. This label says nothing about harm, truth, desirability or repair necessity.
- no: O and C express the same target proposition, including its scope, force and attribution; only surface wording changes. Adding a faithful explicit definition does not itself establish change. Adding an unrelated fact outside the target is outside this field's scope.
- unknown: O/C are provided but their target relation cannot be determined from this limited packet (e.g. unresolved referent, ambiguity, or missing context). Do not import source-only detail into O or guess omitted context.
- not_applicable: there is no original target proposition or no O/C comparison to make. A normal unchanged pair is no, not not_applicable.

Use S only to resolve the supplied terms/context and independently assess factual support; S is not retroactively text in O. For a source-only condition, distinguish “O did not state it” from “C newly contradicted it.” The latter can change O's general proposition by adding a restriction without being an omission of words from O. Record that relation precisely in one short sentence.

`candidate_factual_error` is the local reporting name for the EXISTING factual_error judgment, not a new field in the writer protocol: yes / no / unknown / not_applicable. Yes requires a target C proposition directly contradicted by matching S (subject, time, unit, scope and attribution). No means the supplied evidence supports the local proposition or establishes faithful paraphrase; it never certifies a full article. Unknown means insufficient evidence; absence of evidence is not falsity. Not_applicable is reserved for a purely normative target with no truth-assessable factual or attribution component. A report of who said what is fact-assessable even when the reported opinion cannot be settled objectively.

`normative_value_resolution`: resolved / unresolved / not_applicable. This is a bounded reporting status of the existing value/attribution distinction, not an extra article-review checklist. Use not_applicable when no normative value claim is at issue. Use unresolved when S does not settle the evaluative claim itself; accurate attribution, disagreement, an authority's opinion, or a judge's own preference cannot settle it. Resolved requires an explicit in-packet agreed criterion and evidence that actually settle the stated bounded normative question; name both. Never invent a fairness/preference gold. Keep normative resolution separate from the objectively checkable question of who said what.

Return one JSON object with a rows array in input order. Each row contains only: id, original_proposition_changed, changed_relation (one short sentence), candidate_factual_error, normative_value_resolution, source_anchors (short exact quotes with document_id O/C/S1/etc. and locator). Give O and C anchors for the relation and relevant S anchors for the factual/value distinctions. Report unknown where needed; do not make up evidence. Do not return a normative truth verdict. The replacement label alone never authorizes restoring O or patching C.
